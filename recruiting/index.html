Timed assessment · 30 minutes

Read → design → implement → test

  1. 0–3 min: restate the task and inspect constraints.
  2. 3–5 min: choose the data structure and invariant.
  3. 5–20 min: implement the simplest valid solution.
  4. 20–26 min: test minimum, duplicate, boundary, and failure cases.
  5. 26–29 min: fix, simplify, and confirm complexity.
  6. 29–30 min: verify the signature and submit.

Practical interview

Make the work legible

  1. Clarify: users, inputs, constraints, and error behavior.
  2. Decompose: identify the smallest end-to-end slice.
  3. Choose: explain the model and meaningful tradeoffs.
  4. Build: preserve a working path and narrate decisions.
  5. Verify: cover happy path, empty state, boundaries, and failure.
  6. Reflect: discuss complexity, observability, and the next iteration.

Behavioral + technical depth

Prepare six stories

  • An end-to-end project you shipped and its user impact.
  • A root-cause bug you traced through multiple layers.
  • A blocker you surfaced before it became expensive.
  • A technical tradeoff or disagreement you navigated.
  • A time you improved quality, testing, or observability.
  • Responsible AI-tool use you can fully explain and debug.

Before scheduling

Ask the recruiter

  • What stages and broad competencies are evaluated?
  • Is coding live, take-home, or proctored?
  • Which language, framework, and environment are used?
  • Which references, internet access, or AI tools are permitted?
  • Is system design included at this level?

Reading the evidence

Confidence stays attached

High

Current official source

Job descriptions, assessment documentation, or a first-party intern account. Strong for stated skills and rules.

Medium

Relevant candidate report

Dated, first-person, and role-adjacent. Useful for themes, but not a promise of the current process.

Low

Anonymous or underspecified

Old, hearsay, or missing a role and date. Included only when it adds context and is labeled accordingly.
